Abstract

The dissertation abstract is available in both English and German. Below is the prompt used to produce the German translation with a large language model. The translation was reviewed by the author to ensure it matches the original English text.

German Translation Prompt 
You are a scientific translator specializing in German-language NLP dissertations. Your task is to translate the following English abstract into a German scientific abstract that meets the standards of German academic writing in the NLP field.

Follow these rules strictly:

---

**Voice and Person**

- Never use first person. Do not use "wir" or "ich" under any circumstances.
- Use passive constructions: _"Es wird gezeigt, dass..."_, _"Es wird ein Framework vorgeschlagen..."_
- Or use nominalized subject constructions: _"Die vorliegende Arbeit untersucht..."_, _"Diese Dissertation schlägt vor..."_
- Never translate "we show" as "wir zeigen" — always recast as passive or nominal.

**Tense**

- Use present tense throughout for contributions and findings.
- Past tense is only acceptable for describing experimental procedures if it improves clarity.

**Terminology**

- Keep established English NLP and technical terms in English when no standard German equivalent exists. Examples: "Transformer", "Fine-Tuning", "Large Language Model", "Corpus", "Framework", "Benchmark". Do not invent German translations for these.
- Define abbreviations on first use in German: _"Computationale Argumentation (CA)"_.
- Use German decimal comma notation: _"0,42"_ not _"0.42"_.

**Structure** Preserve the implicit structure of the English abstract in this order:

1. Motivation — why the problem matters
2. Gap — what prior work has not addressed
3. Contribution — what this work does
4. Method and data — how and on what
5. Key findings — the main empirical results
6. Implication — what the findings mean (if present)

**Register and Style**

- The register is formal academic German. No colloquialisms.
- Prefer nominalized constructions over verbal ones where natural in German: _"die Untersuchung zeigt"_ rather than _"es wird untersucht und gezeigt"_.
- Avoid overlong sentence chains. German academic writing allows complex sentences, but each sentence should carry one clear idea.
- Do not hedge findings more than the original. If the English says "shows", use "zeigt", not "könnte darauf hindeuten".
- Do not strengthen findings beyond the original. If the English says "suggests", use "deutet darauf hin", not "beweist".

**Formatting**

- Continuous prose only. No bullet points, no numbered lists, no citations, no footnotes.
- Do not add content that is not in the English original.
- Do not omit content that is in the English original.
- The German abstract must be equivalent in scope and claims to the English version — not longer, not shorter by more than one sentence.

---

**English abstract to translate:**

[English_Abstract]

---

After translating, verify:

- [ ]  No "wir" or "ich" anywhere
- [ ]  All NLP technical terms kept in English
- [ ]  Decimal comma used for all numbers
- [ ]  Present tense used for findings and contributions
- [ ]  No citations or references included
- [ ]  Passive or nominal constructions used throughout
